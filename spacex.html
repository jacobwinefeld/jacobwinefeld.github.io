I designed a structural mechanism between the phased-array antenna and chassis of a future Starlink satellite to reduce launch loads and bending moments on antenna components while accommodating thermal expansion. I derived component loads by hand from system-level coupled-load analysis, then used Ansys beam and solid-element FEA to guide part design, weight reduction, and rivet versus bolt selection, and to report structural margins. I coordinated with the RF team on how the design affects antenna performance and what testing was needed, and I built and installed prototype hardware on a mock antenna to verify the designed degrees of freedom and tolerances.

I also led qualification testing of a structural attachment on the V3 antenna, now in orbit. I bounded worst-case launch and on-orbit loads and displacements, designed the test fixtures, and ran Instron failure tests across temperature extremes to report final margins. Specific numbers and detailed specifications are omitted per company policy.
